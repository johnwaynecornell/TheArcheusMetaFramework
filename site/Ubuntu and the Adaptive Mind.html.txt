        <h3><strong>III. ARF Through the Lens of Ubuntu</strong></h3>
        <p>The <strong>Adaptive Reasoning Framework (ARF)</strong> is designed to respond to complexity by updating belief structures, behaviors, and interpretations in real time. It reflects an entity’s ability to recognize misalignment, reflect meaningfully, and adjust effectively.</p>
        <p>What is striking is that <strong>Ubuntu already performs this framework</strong>—but through embodied, relational, and symbolic gestures rather than abstract formulations. Ubuntu is <strong>ARF in motion</strong>, practiced in ritual, in language, in everyday adaptation.</p>
        <p>Let us examine each <strong>ARF layer</strong> and show how it <strong>manifests within Ubuntu’s symbolic system</strong>:</p>

        <h4>🧠 Recognition → <code>Contextual&nbsp;Identity</code></h4>
        <p>ARF Layer: <strong>Recognition of signal, discrepancy, or new context</strong></p>

        <p><code>Self&nbsp;⊢&nbsp;Group</code>, therefore <strong>context defines the self</strong>, not the other way around.</p>
        <p>Example:</p>
        <p>When a child misbehaves, the question is not “What did <em>you</em> do?”<br>
            It is often: “<em>What happened to us</em> that led to this?”</p>
        <p><strong>Symbolic parallel</strong>:</p>
        <p><code>Signal&nbsp;:=&nbsp;&Delta;(Self_in_Context)</code><br>
            <code>Recognition_Ubuntu&nbsp;:=&nbsp;Detect(Relational&nbsp;Shift)</code></p>

        <h4>🪞 Reflection → <code>Collective&nbsp;Memory&nbsp;and&nbsp;Shared&nbsp;Narrative</code></h4>

        <p>Ubuntu’s reflective process is <strong>oral, communal, and narrative-based</strong>.</p>

        <p>In ARF, a symbolic system reflects on itself.<br>
